Tax warning: this dashboard does not calculate taxes

The numbers below are pre-tax planning estimates. They can materially overstate available cash and retirement income if selling ML or Morgan Stanley stock creates taxable gains, if German tax residency applies, or if German purchase costs must be paid in cash.

U.S. stock-sale tax
Long-term federal capital gains are commonly 0%, 15%, or 20%; high-income taxpayers may also owe 3.8% NIIT, plus possible state tax. Tax applies to gains, not gross sale proceeds.
German purchase costs
Typical buyer-side extra costs can include transfer tax, notary/land registry, and broker commission. A rough stress-test range is 5.5%–15% of purchase price.
German investment/rental tax
German tax residency can change the treatment of investment income, capital gains, and rental income. German capital income often has 25% withholding tax plus solidarity surcharge.

Tax caveats added to this version

  • Stock sales: sale proceeds are not the taxable amount; realized gain equals sale proceeds minus tax basis, adjusted for any wash sales or other rules.
  • U.S. taxes: long-term gains may be taxed at 0%, 15%, or 20%, with possible 3.8% NIIT and state tax.
  • Germany: German tax residency may affect worldwide investment income, stock sales, apartment rental income, and later property sale tax treatment.
  • Purchase costs: German closing costs are not currently deducted from retirement assets or added to loan requirements.
